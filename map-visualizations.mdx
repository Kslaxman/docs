# Map Visualizations

Understanding spatial demand visually requires mapping algorithms that can seamlessly handle millions of GPS coordinates without completely stalling the graphics rendering engine. File `03map_visualizations.ipynb` covers our exploration of various dynamic web-mapping techniques.

## High-Density Coordinate Plotting

### Demand Heatmaps
Our core visualization involves mapping a smooth gradient heatmap representing pickup density for millions of rides.
- We leverage `Folium` and `PyDeck` to draw accurate heat layers.
- The map includes a color legend explaining intensity levels, scaling from "cool" low-activity suburban zones directly into the intensely active "hot" downtown zones over the Loop.

### Dynamic Temporal Views
Demand clusters shift based on temporal variables. We built iterative visualizations including:
- **Hourly Heatmap Cycling**: Maps rendered through a time-slider looping all 24 hours of the day.
- **Weekday vs. Weekend Heatmaps**: Revealing clear geographical displacements. Weekdays center entirely on commercial zones; Weekends diffuse heavily into nightlife localized bars and northern residential districts.

## Spatial Context Additives
Alongside standard plotting, the maps dynamically display calculated aggregates:
- We inject custom HTML Context Boxes (`add_context_box`) on the rendering canvas. These floating UIs output live statistical facts (average fares, trip counts, active duration clusters) specifically scoped to the geometry bounded on the screen.

### High-Fidelity Scatter Mapping
While heatmaps are extremely advantageous for finding spatial anomalies, they act as smoothing metrics. 
To preserve local variance, we implemented Scatter Maps that process downsampled clusters (~500,000 trips) plotting every single pickup as a discrete, color-coded node based on the time of day, thereby eliminating spatial dilution.
